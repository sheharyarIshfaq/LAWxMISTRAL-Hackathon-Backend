regarding quantifiying the harm = IS THE HARM QUANTIFIED?

Classify the harm into one of three levels by answering these questions in order, using only the decision:

1. Does the decision, a statute or a contract fix the amount? → Quantified.
   (e.g. statutory late-payment penalties, an amount wrongly collected stated in the decision)
2. If not, can the amount be calculated from objective figures with a known method, without the judge's discretion? → Quantifiable.
   (e.g. sales value × overcharge in a cartel, gap between the displayed and the real price, interest to be refunded, documented fraud losses)
3. If not, does the existence or the amount of the harm depend on the judge's assessment or on proof by each victim? → To be proven.
   (e.g. non-material harm, anxiety, loss of chance, loss of control over personal data)

Rules:
1. Wording such as "de nature à créer un préjudice" (likely to cause harm), "risque" (risk) or "susceptible de" (liable to) describes a potential harm, not an established one: it points to "To be proven".
2. If the authority admits uncertainty about causation (e.g. "pas possible de déterminer avec certitude"), quote it: it confirms "To be proven".
3. For data protection cases, an infringement of the GDPR is not enough: each victim must prove damage (CJEU case law). Default to "To be proven" unless the decision gives figures.
4. If sub-groups differ, give a level for each. Example: "To be proven for all subscribers (non-material harm, § 82); Quantifiable for victims of proven fraud (bank statements), subject to proving the link with the breach (§ 84)."
5. Justify the level in one or two sentences, quoting the decision with its paragraph number. Never invent an amount.
